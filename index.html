Precision is a property of the ticket, not a property of the company

A brokerage fractional share, where offered, is usually measured as a fraction of one share on a securities statement. A tokenized ticket may show a decimal because the venue allows a quantity step, or because the order is quoted in USDT or in another currency the account supports. Official materials note that order-book pairs are commonly quoted in USDT, while buy/sell/convert may use other quote currencies. Those two kinds of decimal do not share an official conversion formula, and this article will not invent one. Fees and minimums are whatever the confirmation shows. A finer increment does not make an off-hours mid closer to the official close, and it does not reduce the need to label sources.

Eligibility does not become easier when the step is small. You have to be able to make the representations in the terms, and review may still be required. A product-page snapshot is not your list. Binance bStocks is a different product and may use a different precision; do not paste one venue’s increment onto the other, and do not treat either registration as a traditional brokerage opening. If you trade outside regular U.S. cash-market hours — NYSE materials describe 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern, subject to that day’s calendar — official materials still warn that spreads may widen and that a gap may appear at the reopen. A more precise number can still sit inside a wider spread.

Record size the way the ticket writes it

  • Confirm eligibility before you debate precision.
  • On the confirmation, write down the code, the unit name, the minimum step, the quantity, and the fees — not only how many digits you see.
  • Do not divide a dollar news price into a USDT balance and call the result a share count.
  • Off hours, accept the spread first, then decide whether a limit is appropriate.
  • Do not copy a size method onto Binance bStocks, and do not describe around-the-clock matching as a traditional T+0 rule.

Frequently asked questions

If the screen lets me enter a very small amount, is that fractional stock trading?

Usually not in the brokerage sense. It is a venue unit for price exposure.

Does a longer decimal mean I am closer to the “real” price?

No. Precision is a step. It does not eliminate a spread.

Can I reuse a broker’s fractional quantity here?

No. The confirmation governs.

Is precision the same on Binance bStocks?

Do not assume that. Read that venue’s confirmation.
