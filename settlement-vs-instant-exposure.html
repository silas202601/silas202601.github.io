Give each clock a name, then refuse to let them share a verb

In a traditional securities account, settlement is the post-trade process in which cash and shares are delivered under market rules. The cycle can change, so this article will not freeze a “T+n” number as a permanent standard. Readers should check the current official rules of the exchange, the clearing system, and the broker. On a tokenized ticket, “filled” first means that the venue matched price exposure under its own rules. It tells you whether the order was accepted, not that cash-market settlement is complete, and not that you have been written onto a shareholder register. Folding the two clocks into “instant stock settlement” is a misuse of the term.

The distinction is easier to miss after the cash close. NYSE materials describe a regular session of 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern, subject to that day’s calendar. After that session, a tokenized book and a settlement calendar can appear in the same evening without becoming the same event. Official materials warn that off-hours spreads may widen and that a gap may appear at the reopen. Eligibility still comes first: you have to be able to make the representations in the terms. Official materials note that order-book pairs are commonly quoted in USDT. Binance bStocks is a different product, and its matching language has to be read separately. Neither venue should be described as a traditional brokerage opening or as instant settlement.

Frequently asked questions

Why does this guide not just tell me whether settlement is now T+1?

Because cash-market rules can change, and because that cycle is not the same thing as a tokenized match. Official materials at the time govern.

If I can use the proceeds right away on the venue, is that instant settlement?

Account permissions about reuse are venue rules. They should not be written as completed market settlement.

Does a fill put me on a shareholder settlement timetable?

Not by default. You hold price exposure.

Is the settlement wording the same on Binance bStocks?

Do not assume that. Read Binance’s documents.
