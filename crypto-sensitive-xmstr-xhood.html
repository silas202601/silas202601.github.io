Split the reasons the two names are discussed, then split the tickets

Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy, with the cash ticker still often written MSTR) is frequently discussed next to bitcoin the company discloses. xMSTR tracks the stock’s price; it does not deliver those coins, and it does not make a weekend coin print equal the official cash close. Robinhood (HOOD) is a listed brokerage firm. xHOOD tracks that firm’s share price; it does not open a customer account there, and it does not let you trade other U.S. names inside that firm’s app. Treating “crypto-sensitive stocks” as one basket stacks two company stories and two rights expectations that do not belong in a single confirmation.

Eligibility is one door; the live list is not a pair. You have to be able to make the representations in the terms, and review may still be required. If one code is missing today, the other is not a substitute. A product-page snapshot is not your personal pair. Official materials note that order-book pairs are commonly quoted in USDT, while other modes may use currencies the account supports; each confirmation has its own fees and size. Binance bStocks is a different product; do not migrate the two names as a portable bundle. If you trade outside regular U.S. cash-market hours — NYSE materials describe 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern, subject to that day’s calendar — both mids may diverge, and they need not diverge in the same direction.

Frequently asked questions

Because both names appear in crypto discussions, are they an official pair?

No. They are two independent price-exposure tickets.

If I buy xMSTR, do I indirectly hold bitcoin?

No. You track the stock’s price, not the assets the company discloses.

If I buy xHOOD, do I enter Robinhood’s trading system?

No. xHOOD is not a brokerage opening.

Can I place both as one package on Binance bStocks?

Do not assume that. Look them up separately on Binance’s list.
